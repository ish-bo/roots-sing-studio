# Roots Music Collective

what are the pain points for NRI adults to learn classical Indian music and karaoke songs

NRI (Non-Resident Indian) adults often want to reconnect with their culture, but learning classical Indian music and Bollywood karaoke singing comes with unique challenges. The pain points are somewhat different for each, though there is overlap.

Pain Points for Learning Classical Indian Music (Carnatic/Hindustani)

1. Finding Qualified Teachers in Their Time Zone

Most expert teachers are based in India, creating scheduling difficulties.

Lessons often happen early morning or late at night.

Limited access to in-person guidance and correction.

Impact: Inconsistent learning and slower progress.

2. Lack of Structured Learning Paths for Adults

Most classical music programs are designed for:

Children

Serious performers

Long-term disciples

Adults usually want:

Flexible learning

Faster progress

Clear milestones

Hobby-oriented instruction

Pain point: "I don't want to become a professional musician; I just want to sing well."

3. Fear of Starting Too Late

Many NRIs believe:

"Classical music should have started in childhood."

"My voice isn't trained."

"I don't know the basics."

This creates:

Imposter syndrome

Fear of embarrassment

Hesitation to join classes with younger students

4. Difficulty Understanding Indian Musical Concepts

Concepts like:

Ragas

Shruti

Gamakas

Tala cycles

Manodharma (improvisation)

can feel abstract, especially if:

They didn't grow up in India

They don't know Indian languages

Their musical exposure is mostly Bollywood or Western music

5. Lack of Daily Practice Discipline

NRI adults often juggle:

Full-time jobs

Parenting

Elder care

Household responsibilities

Classical music requires:

Daily riyaz

Repetition

Listening practice

Pain point: Consistency is difficult.

6. Pronunciation and Language Barriers

Classical compositions are often in:

Sanskrit

Telugu

Tamil

Kannada

Braj Bhasha

Hindi/Urdu

Second-generation NRIs may struggle with:

Pronunciation

Meaning

Emotional expression

7. Limited Cultural Context

Understanding:

Bhakti traditions

Mythological references

Composer histories

Raga moods

adds depth to learning.

Many NRIs feel:

"I can sing the notes, but I don't understand the soul of the composition."

8. Lack of Community

In India, students can:

Attend concerts

Participate in group practice

Join music circles

NRIs often learn in isolation.

This reduces:

Motivation

Accountability

Performance opportunities

9. Technology Friction

Common issues:

Poor audio quality on Zoom

Latency preventing simultaneous singing

Difficulty receiving pitch correction online

Inability to hear subtle gamakas

10. Progress Feels Slow

Classical music is inherently long-term:

Months for alankars

Years for improvisation

Decades for mastery

Adults often expect quicker wins and become discouraged.

Pain Points for Learning Bollywood/Karaoke Singing

1. Wanting to Sing Specific Songs Quickly

Most adults want:

"Teach me to sing this Kishore Kumar song."

"I want to perform at a party."

They don't necessarily want:

Years of classical training.

Traditional teachers may insist on:

"First learn Sarali Varisai or Alankars for six months."

This creates a mismatch.

2. Difficulty Matching Pitch

Common issues:

Singing off-key

Not identifying notes

Inability to find the starting note (scale)

Many adults have never received ear training.

3. Breath Control Problems

Bollywood songs often involve:

Long phrases

High notes

Dynamic expression

Adults may:

Run out of breath

Sound strained

Lose control during difficult passages

4. Choosing the Right Scale

A huge pain point:

"The original singer's scale is too high for me."

Many don't know:

Their vocal range

How to transpose karaoke tracks

Which key suits their voice

5. Lack of Constructive Feedback

Most practice involves:

Singing alone

Family saying "You sound good"

No one explains:

Why notes are flat

Timing issues

Voice placement problems

6. Stage Fear and Performance Anxiety

Many adults want to:

Sing at family gatherings

Participate in community events

Join singing competitions

Fears include:

Forgetting lyrics

Singing off-key publicly

Being judged by friends and relatives

7. Difficulty with Ornamentation

Bollywood songs frequently use:

Murkis

Meend

Vibrato

Classical touches

Learners can sing the melody but struggle to reproduce the singer's expression.

8. Language and Diction Issues

Second-generation NRIs may struggle with:

Hindi/Urdu pronunciation

Regional language songs

Emotional delivery

9. Lack of Time

Typical NRI schedule:

Tech jobs

Long commutes

Young children

Multiple responsibilities

They want:

15–20 minute practice sessions

Flexible scheduling

Measurable progress

10. No Safe Space for Beginners

Adults often feel:

"Everyone else sings better than me."

They want:

Judgment-free communities

Beginner groups

Opportunities to practice before public performances

Emotional NeedExampleCultural reconnection"I want my children to see me sing Indian songs."Identity"I don't want to lose my roots."Stress relief"Music is my therapy after work."Social belonging"I want to sing with other Indians."Personal achievement"I always wanted to learn, but never had the chance."Confidence"I want to perform once on stage."
